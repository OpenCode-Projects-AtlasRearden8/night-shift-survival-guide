Glossary

Plain-English definitions for the words this manual uses. Education-only definitions — the kind a friendly nurse would give you, not dictionary law or clinical documentation.

Anchor sleep — A block of sleep kept at roughly the same clock time every single day, including days off. The keystone habit of night-shift sleep planning; it prevents your entire schedule from drifting day by day.

Blackout curtains — Curtains (or liners, or portable panels) rated to block nearly all outside light. The highest-payoff single purchase for sleeping in daylight.

Caffeine half-life — The time it takes your body to clear about half of the caffeine you drank. Typically in the 3–7 hour range for adults, varying by person and situation — which is why caffeine late in a shift can still be affecting you hours into your sleep window.

Circadian rhythm — Your internal daily clock: the roughly 24-hour cycle that coordinates sleepiness, alertness, temperature, appetite and more. It takes its cues mainly from light exposure.

Coffee nap — The combination of drinking coffee immediately before a short nap (usually 20 minutes or so). The idea: caffeine takes roughly 20–30 minutes to begin taking effect, so you wake around when it starts working. A minutes-level tool, not a cure for sleep debt.

Core body temperature — Your body's internal temperature, which naturally eases downward as sleep approaches and rises near morning. A cool, dark room works with this process rather than against it.

Day-recovery drift — The tendency of a night worker's schedule to slide later and later across a stretch of nights, usually because "off" days have no structure. Fought with anchor sleep and deliberate light exposure.

Do Not Disturb (DND) / call-twice rule — A phone and household convention: silence all notifications, but two calls in a row from the same number breaks through. A cheap emergency-priority system.

Light hygiene — The deliberate management of light timing: bright light when you want alertness, low light and darkness before your sleep window. Includes sunglasses on the drive home after nights.

Microsleep — A brief involuntary episode of sleep, lasting up to a few seconds, that you are usually not aware of. The main reason drowsy driving is dangerous: a few seconds of sleep at highway speed travels the length of a football field in the dark.

Night float / night rotation — Schedule patterns in which staff work nights for a set stretch (three nights, seven nights, rotating fortnights and so on). This manual's planner supports all of them.

Pre-shift nap — A planned nap taken before a night shift, typically 20–30 or 90 minutes. Different durations behave differently; see the chapter on sleep-window planning.

Recovery day — A designated low-energy day off after a night stretch, built to restore rather than catch up: slow morning light, one anchor task, gentle movement, a real evening. See the recovery day chapter.

Sleep debt — The cumulative gap between sleep need and sleep actually obtained over days or weeks. It is not repaid in one heroic sleep and it is usually the real cause of "I suddenly can't handle 3 a.m. anymore."

Sleep inertia — Grogginess and disorientation right after waking, especially from a mid-cycle awakening during a nap. Fades within minutes; longer naps in an awkward window make it more likely.

Sleep window — Your protected daily block for sleep: fixed start and end times, defended with household agreements, phone rules, and physical environment. The central planning unit of this manual.

Shift-cycle planner — The worksheet page of this manual: one table per cycle mapping shifts, naps, sleep windows, caffeine cutoffs and reviews. See the planner worksheet.

White noise / brown noise — Steady sound used to mask disruptive environmental noise; white noise spreads evenly across frequencies, while brown noise concentrates on lower frequencies and sounds deeper. Either one, played on a speaker at modest volume, does the masking job.

Wind-down period — The last 30–60 minutes before your sleep window, deliberately dim, dull, and screen-light. A ritual, not luck.
